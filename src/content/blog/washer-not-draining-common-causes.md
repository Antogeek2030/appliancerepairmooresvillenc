Few laundry problems are as immediately disruptive as a washer full of water that refuses to drain. Before assuming the worst, a few straightforward checks often reveal the cause — and sometimes resolve it.

## Start With the Obvious

**Lid or door switch.** Many washers will not complete a drain or spin cycle if the lid (top-load) or door (front-load) is not registering as closed. Make sure nothing is obstructing the latch and that the door closes firmly.

**Cycle selection.** Confirm you didn't accidentally select a no-spin or delicate cycle that limits draining. Running a normal or drain/spin cycle can sometimes clear a temporary glitch.

## Check the Drain Path

**Drain hose.** Look for kinks where the hose leaves the machine or enters the standpipe or utility sink. Straighten any sharp bends. Also confirm the hose isn't pushed too far down the standpipe (which can create a siphon effect or restrict flow).

**Lint and debris.** Over time, small items (coins, buttons, lint clumps) can accumulate in the drain pump filter or trap. Many front-loaders and some top-loaders have an accessible filter near the bottom front — consult your manual for the location and how to clean it safely (water will come out).

## Listen for the Pump

When you select a drain cycle, you should hear the pump run. Silence may indicate an electrical issue, a failed pump, or a control problem. A humming sound without water movement often points to a jammed impeller.

## Other Frequent Culprits

- **Clogged house drain.** If other fixtures are draining slowly, the problem may be further downstream than the washer.
- **Faulty lid switch or door lock.** These safety components fail regularly and are usually replaceable.
- **Pump failure.** Impellers wear out or motors seize; replacement is a common repair.

## Safety Notes

Always unplug the machine before removing panels or filters. If water is deep in the drum, you may need to manually bail or use a wet/dry vacuum carefully. Avoid forcing components.

If the basic checks don't restore draining, or if you're uncomfortable with the filter or hose access, a service visit can diagnose pump, control, or wiring issues efficiently.
