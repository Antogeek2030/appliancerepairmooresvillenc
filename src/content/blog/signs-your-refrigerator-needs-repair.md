A refrigerator that starts acting up rarely fails without some earlier clues. Catching those signs early can save food, reduce stress, and sometimes keep a repair simpler. Here are five common indicators worth paying attention to.

## 1. Temperature Fluctuations

If milk spoils faster than usual or frozen items develop ice crystals or soft spots, the cooling system may be struggling. Check that vents inside the fridge aren't blocked by containers, then monitor temperatures with an appliance thermometer if you have one. Persistent swings outside the recommended ranges (generally around 37°F for the fridge and 0°F for the freezer) often point to a component issue.

## 2. Unusual Noises

Modern refrigerators make some sound — compressors and fans are normal. New grinding, rattling, or clicking that wasn't there before can signal a failing fan motor, loose part, or compressor under stress. Note when the noise occurs (during cooling cycles, when the door closes, etc.) so a technician can focus the diagnosis.

## 3. Excess Frost or Condensation

Frost buildup in a frost-free freezer, or condensation forming inside the fresh-food section, can indicate a defrost system problem, a door seal that isn't sealing, or restricted airflow. A simple first step is inspecting the door gasket for tears or debris and cleaning the condenser coils (usually at the bottom or back) if they're dusty.

## 4. Water on the Floor

Puddles near the front or inside the unit often come from a clogged defrost drain. Sometimes ice blocks the drain path; other times a cracked drain pan or water filter housing is the culprit. Addressing leaks promptly protects flooring and cabinets.

## 5. Rising Energy Use or Longer Run Times

If the compressor seems to run almost constantly, or your energy bill climbs without another explanation, the unit may be working harder than it should. Dirty coils, failing seals, or a weak compressor are common contributors.

## When to Call for Help

Some issues respond to basic maintenance — coil cleaning, level adjustments, clearing vents. Others benefit from professional diagnostics, especially sealed-system concerns or electrical faults.
